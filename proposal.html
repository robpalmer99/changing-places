Why it matters

What it takes to turn a click into a buyer

Ask an AI tool for a real estate website and it will build you one. What it gives a first-time builder is the layout it gives everyone: a big headline in the middle, three boxes with icons, and a contact form. Your buyers have seen that page a thousand times, and it doesn't say "this agency is different".

The harder problem is knowing what to ask it for. An AI tool doesn't know which words make a cautious couple in their sixties trust a new agency with a $300,000 decision. It doesn't know what a listing ad has to say to earn the click, how to tell which ad produced which enquiry, or what to send someone who signed up for alerts and then went quiet. Those answers come from experience. The tool only does as well as the person directing it.

Here's the whole journey a buyer takes before they're standing in one of your properties:

  1. They see your ad for a property on Facebook.
  2. They land on a page about that property.This is the website
  3. They tap WhatsApp or LINE, or ask for new-listing alerts.
  4. They get a quick, warm reply in their own language.
  5. They book a viewing, and you take it from there.

A website on its own covers step two. This proposal sets up all five.

The plan

What you get

Everything you need to go from a Facebook ad to a conversation with a buyer, built and live in about three weeks.

  1. Your website, finished and live

    The design you've seen, completed with your real listings, your story and your five areas, and live on changingplacesrealty.com. It's built to be fast on the phones and tablets your buyers browse on in the evening. Listings run from a simple spreadsheet, so adding a property, cutting a price or marking one sold means editing a single row.

  2. Words that sell, written by me

    Every page: the homepage, your story, a short guide to each of your five areas, and the template every listing follows. All of it is written for a wealthy couple deciding whether to trust a new agency with the biggest purchase of their retirement.

  3. A page for every listing ad

    When someone clicks your ad for a Pratumnak condo, they land on that condo instead of your homepage. The WhatsApp and LINE buttons open with a message already filled in that names the property, so you know exactly what they're asking about before you reply.

  4. Your first Facebook ads

    Ad copy for your first three listings, with several versions of each so you can see which one buyers respond to, plus a template for every listing after that. You, or whoever runs your ads, posts them.

  5. Tracking

    The Meta pixel and conversion tracking, set up so Facebook learns what kind of person messages you and finds more of them, and so you can see which ad brought in which enquiry.

  6. Follow-up

    A new-listing alert sign-up connected to a free email tool, with a short welcome series that keeps you in mind while buyers are still months from deciding. Plus English reply templates for WhatsApp, for the first message and the follow-ups, so every enquiry gets a fast, consistent answer.

  7. The parts nobody sees

    The domain, hosting, contact forms that actually reach your inbox, a privacy notice that meets Thailand's data protection law (PDPA), a Google Business Profile, and the groundwork that lets Google and AI assistants like ChatGPT read your listings and recommend you.
